Constitution

Fundamental Rights

Fundamental Rights are constitutionally protected rights provided to individuals under Part III of the Constitution.

ARTICLE 14–18

Right to Equality

Provides constitutional guarantees concerning equality before law and equal protection of laws.

ARTICLE 19–22

Right to Freedom

Includes important freedoms and protections subject to constitutional limitations.

ARTICLE 23–24

Right Against Exploitation

Protects against trafficking, forced labour and certain forms of child labour.

ARTICLE 25–28

Freedom of Religion

Provides constitutional protection concerning freedom of conscience and religion.

ARTICLE 29–30

Cultural & Educational Rights

Protects certain cultural and educational interests, particularly of minorities.

ARTICLE 32

Constitutional Remedies

Provides a constitutional mechanism for seeking enforcement of Fundamental Rights.

Legal Awareness

Common legal terms explained in simple language.

What is an FIR?

An FIR is a formal record made by police regarding information relating to a cognizable offence.

What is Bail?

Bail generally refers to release from custody subject to conditions imposed under applicable law.

What is an Affidavit?

An affidavit is a written statement of facts confirmed by the person making it under oath or affirmation.

What is RTI?

RTI refers to the legal framework allowing citizens to seek information from public authorities, subject to exemptions.

What is a Legal Notice?

A legal notice is a formal communication that may inform another party about a claim or intended legal action.

What is a Complaint?

A complaint is a formal expression of a grievance or allegation made before an appropriate authority.
